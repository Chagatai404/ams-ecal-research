# Chaos, Fractals, Multifractals, and QML — Research Framing

This document records the conceptual research thread that motivated the current multifractal AMS-02 ECAL hypothesis and clarifies what is in scope now versus later.

The purpose is to preserve the reasoning without overstating what has been demonstrated.

---

## 1. Core distinction

The project must keep two ideas separate:

### Fractal / multifractal shower structure

This is an **active physics hypothesis** for AMS ECAL.

Particle showers are branching stochastic cascades. Their spatial energy deposition may exhibit scale-dependent structure that can be described by effective fractal or multifractal observables over the finite ECAL resolution.

Candidate observables include:

- box-counting-style dimension \(D_0\);
- information dimension \(D_1\);
- correlation dimension \(D_2\);
- generalized dimensions \(D_q\);
- partition-function scaling \(Z_q(\epsilon)\);
- scaling exponent \(\tau(q)\);
- multifractal spectrum \(f(\alpha)\);
- lacunarity;
- entropy / concentration across scales.

The immediate scientific question is whether such observables differ between electromagnetic and proton-induced showers **after controlling energy, geometry, detector granularity, and preprocessing choices**.

### Deterministic chaos

This is **not currently an AMS shower claim**.

A classical chaotic dynamical system usually assumes a deterministic evolution such as

\[
x_{t+1}=F(x_t)
\]

or a continuous flow

\[
\dot{x}=F(x)
\]

with sensitivity to initial conditions, often characterized using quantities such as Lyapunov exponents.

AMS ECAL shower development is primarily a stochastic branching process generated by particle interactions. Therefore the project must not infer "chaos" merely from visual complexity, branching, irregularity, or fractal-looking geometry.

Any future claim that a shower is chaotic must specify:

- the state variable;
- the evolution law;
- the dynamical parameter;
- the observable used to diagnose chaos;
- why the stochastic interaction process can legitimately be treated within that dynamical-systems framework.

Until then, chaos theory is a **mathematical/computational toolbox and adjacent research direction**, not an established shower mechanism.

## 2. Why fractals are physically plausible for particle showers

A shower develops through repeated branching interactions.

For an electromagnetic cascade, schematically:

\[
e^\pm \rightarrow e^\pm + \gamma
\]

followed by

\[
\gamma \rightarrow e^- + e^+
\]

and subsequent repetitions until ionization losses dominate.

Hadronic showers contain additional nuclear interactions and electromagnetic subshowers, for example through neutral-pion decay.

This produces a cascade whose morphology can vary across spatial scales.

The project hypothesis is not that an ECAL shower is an ideal infinite mathematical fractal. AMS has:

- finite depth;
- finite transverse segmentation;
- thresholds;
- detector boundaries;
- leakage;
- a limited set of available scales.

Therefore we should speak of **effective multiscale or multifractal structure at detector resolution**, and explicitly study finite-resolution bias.

## 3. Why multifractals are more useful than one fractal dimension

A single box-counting dimension compresses the shower to one scaling number.

Energy deposition is not only about occupied geometry; it is also about how energy is distributed across occupied regions.

For a partition at scale \(\epsilon\), define normalized energy weights

\[
p_i(\epsilon)=\frac{E_i}{\sum_j E_j}.
\]

Then a generalized partition function can be written as

\[
Z_q(\epsilon)=\sum_i p_i(\epsilon)^q.
\]

If a scaling regime exists,

\[
Z_q(\epsilon)\sim \epsilon^{\tau(q)}.
\]

For \(q\neq 1\),

\[
D_q=\frac{\tau(q)}{q-1}.
\]

Different \(q\) values emphasize different parts of the energy distribution:

- lower \(q\) emphasizes weak / sparse regions;
- higher \(q\) emphasizes dense / energetic regions.

This is potentially valuable for electromagnetic-versus-hadronic discrimination because two showers may have similar total energy and width while distributing energy differently across scales.

All estimators must be validated under AMS finite granularity before physical interpretation.

## 4. Relationship to the current FastMC

The multifractal study depends on realistic event populations.

The current FastMC contains deterministic mean longitudinal and lateral electromagnetic shower models.

The intended progression is:

```text
mean deterministic shower
        ↓
Stochastic generation stochastic event generation
        ↓
Detector response / digitization
        ↓
Dataset generation validated event datasets
        ↓
multiscale / multifractal characterization
```

This is why multifractal learning begins now, while the actual AMS-specific statistical experiment waits for sufficiently validated event populations.

The deterministic mean profile itself is not enough to demonstrate event-level multifractal class separation.

## 5. The first multifractal research program

### RQ-001

Do electron/positron and proton showers exhibit measurably different effective multifractal structure in AMS ECAL after controlling for primary energy and incidence geometry?

### H-001

Electromagnetic and proton-induced showers have different scale-dependent spatial energy distributions, measurable through selected multifractal observables.

### Important competing explanations

Apparent separation may actually be caused by:

- energy differences;
- incidence-angle differences;
- shower containment / leakage;
- finite cell granularity;
- thresholds;
- preprocessing;
- longitudinal shower depth;
- conventional width/concentration variables.

Therefore later analysis must ask not only:

\[
I(Y;F_{\text{multifractal}})>0
\]

but more strongly whether the multifractal features add information conditional on conventional shower observables:

\[
I(Y;F_{\text{multifractal}}\mid F_{\text{conventional}})>0.
\]

This is a conceptual target; the exact estimator/test will be chosen later.

## 6. Relationship to machine learning

Multifractal analysis can be used at several levels.

### A. Hand-engineered physics features

Use quantities such as

\[
[D_0,D_1,D_2,\text{lacunarity},\ldots]
\]

as inputs to simple interpretable models.

This is the first ML baseline because it tests whether the representation itself carries useful information.

### B. Learnable multiscale representation

Instead of computing only fixed summary values, a neural model can process shower information at several coarse-graining scales.

Conceptually:

```text
fine ECAL structure
      ↓
coarse scale 1
      ↓
coarse scale 2
      ↓
coarse scale 3
      ↓
learned scale-dependent representation
```

This can be compared against fixed multifractal estimators.

### C. Geometry-aware representation

AMS ECAL has alternating X/Y readout orientations, so the detector is not simply a generic image.

A future graph or detector-topology-aware model may represent:

- energy;
- physical coordinates;
- layer;
- fiber/readout orientation;
- adjacency / neighborhood across layers.

A possible later architecture is:

```text
geometry-aware branch
        +
multiscale / multifractal branch
        ↓
particle classifier
```

This remains a candidate model until the physics observables are validated.

## 7. Relationship to QML

QML is a computational method, not the reason the fractal hypothesis should be true.

The project should distinguish three separate connections.

### 7.1 Classical multifractal data → quantum feature learning

A compact physically motivated feature vector such as

\[
\mathbf{x}=
[D_0,D_1,D_2,\text{lacunarity},\text{depth},\text{width},\ldots]
\]

could be encoded into a variational quantum classifier or quantum kernel.

This would provide a principled low-dimensional input rather than arbitrary compression of hundreds of ECAL cells.

The correct research question is:

> Does a specific quantum model provide a useful inductive bias or resource advantage for this validated low-dimensional representation compared with fair classical baselines?

No advantage is assumed.

### 7.2 QCNN as a multiscale / coarse-graining architecture

A QCNN has a hierarchical convolution/pooling structure.

That makes it conceptually compatible with a multiscale shower problem because both care about information that survives progressive coarse-graining.

The motivating connection is:

```text
fractal scaling
      ↕
coarse-graining / renormalization ideas
      ↕
hierarchical representations
      ↕
QCNN-style pooling
```

This does **not** mean fractals are quantum or that QCNNs automatically suit showers.

It suggests a testable inductive-bias hypothesis:

> Can a hierarchical quantum circuit learn physically meaningful scale-dependent shower correlations more efficiently or robustly than matched classical hierarchical controls?

This belongs after the multiscale representation is established.

### 7.3 Quantum chaos / quantum reservoir computing

This is a separate future research branch.

Quantum dynamical systems can transition between more ordered/integrable and chaotic regimes, and quantum reservoir computing can use many-body dynamics as a computational substrate.

This connects to the broader chaos research interest, but it is **not required for the first AMS multifractal/QML study**.

Do not mix this into the initial AMS experiment unless a clear research question emerges.

## 8. Relationship to the earlier Hamiltonian-embedding idea

A previous project idea was to encode detector geometry into a Hamiltonian such as

\[
H=
\sum_i h_i Z_i+
\sum_{ij}J_{ij}Z_iZ_j.
\]

The multifractal/multiscale framing makes this idea more precise.

Possible future couplings \(J_{ij}\) could represent:

- physical adjacency;
- inter-layer relationships;
- readout orientation;
- local / intermediate / global scale relationships.

A future quantum feature map might deliberately organize couplings by scale rather than using an arbitrary hardware-efficient ansatz.

This remains a candidate QML mechanism, not an accepted architecture.

## 9. Chaos-theory learning roadmap

The broader chaos/fractal learning roadmap should remain available because it may later produce useful mathematical tools or separate projects.

Suggested conceptual path:

```text
logistic map
→ fixed points and bifurcations
→ Lyapunov exponents
→ Sharkovskii / Li–Yorke chaos
→ Hausdorff and other fractal dimensions
→ strange attractors
→ ergodic theory
→ spatiotemporal chaos
→ quantum chaos
```

For the AMS project, the immediately relevant subset is:

```text
scaling laws
→ fractal dimension
→ generalized dimensions
→ multifractals
→ finite-resolution estimation
→ multiscale representation learning
```

Lyapunov exponents and strange attractors are not prerequisites for the first AMS multifractal experiment.

## 10. Possible future neural-network research outside AMS

A separate research direction discussed alongside this project is biologically inspired recurrent networks operating near critical / edge-of-chaos regimes.

Potential questions include:

- whether adaptive neuronal time constants push recurrent networks toward criticality;
- how the largest Lyapunov exponent relates to memory and generalization;
- whether attractor dimension correlates with computational capability;
- whether sparse excitatory/inhibitory recurrent topology can self-organize near a critical regime.

This is intellectually related through nonlinear dynamics, chaos, and fractal dimension, but it is a **separate research program** from AMS ECAL and should not be allowed to expand the AMS scope prematurely.

## 11. Scientific boundaries

The project must avoid the following shortcuts:

### Do not say

> "Particle showers are chaotic because they look irregular."

### Do not say

> "Particle showers are quantum, therefore QML should work better."

Microscopic interactions are quantum, but ECAL measurements are classical detector observables. This does not imply a QML advantage.

### Do not say

> "QCNNs perform renormalization, therefore they are optimal for fractal showers."

The analogy motivates an experiment; it is not evidence.

### Do say

> "Particle showers are stochastic branching cascades with potentially useful scale-dependent spatial structure."

### Do say

> "Multifractal observables and multiscale neural architectures provide testable representations of that structure."

### Do say

> "Quantum models will be compared only after the representation is physically and statistically justified."
