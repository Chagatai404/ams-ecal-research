# S4 part 1: 3D multiscale estimator controls and eligibility on the fine voxels (2026-10-16) — NEGATIVE under the registered rules

Registration (before any control was run): `research/plans/2026-10-16_s4a_3d_estimator_controls_registration.md`. Code `src/ams_ecal/multiscale_estimators/multiscale_3d.py` (9 tests: scales tile the mesh, the flat index unravels, a uniformly filled mesh gives D = 3 exactly at every window and q, a single voxel gives D = 0, repeated voxels add energy, the samplers stay inside the mesh, the coarse isotropic cascade levels are recovered within 1% at 200,000 hits, the spread statistic separates the cascade from the smooth map at the usable scales). Results `results/multiscale_controls/s4a_3d_controls.json`. Synthetic controls (smooth baseline and 4x4x1-root multiplicative cascade on the 216 x 216 x 36 mesh), 1,000 replicates per hit count and map, hit counts 100 to 30,000, the DEC-013 numbers unchanged (95% baseline interval, false positives at most 10%, power and ordering at least 90%, recovery reference 15%). Exposed development events for the eligibility count only (occupied fine voxels); no D_q of real events was computed; no sealed set read.

## 1. Result

- **One of the 36 windows is usable**: scales (6,4) to (27,18), effective sides 18 to 82 mm, **only at 30,000 hits**, i.e. at a median of **23,721 occupied voxels** of the control images. No window is usable at any lower hit count (100, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000).
- All 36 windows pass the false-positive limit at every hit count. The detection power (cascade D0 - D2 above the baseline's 97.5th percentile) is at least 0.90 for 23 to 31 of the 36 windows. **The ordering rule D0 >= D1 >= D2 in at least 90% of the cascade replicates is what fails**: it holds for 1, 1, 2, 4, 5 and 8 windows at 100, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000 and 30,000 hits, and where it holds at 30,000 hits the power is zero for all but one window. Windows that contain the fine scales are saturated (more boxes than hits: 93,000 boxes of the (3,2) scale against 30,000 hits), which lowers the occupied-box count D0 below D1 and D2.
- **Recovery** of the exact cascade dimensions across the aligned isotropic levels (reference tolerance 15%): at 30,000 hits the coarse levels are within 3.8% and the finest isotropic level (3,2) to (9,6) is 44% off (saturation); at 100 hits the coarse levels are 83% off. The reference is met only for the coarse levels and only at the high hit counts.
- **Real events: no development event is eligible for the one usable window.** Median occupied fine voxels: electrons 3,922 (10 GeV), 6,340 (20), 11,208 (50), 16,505 (100); interacting protons 1,988, 3,063, 5,060, 7,112. The threshold is 23,721. The eligible fraction is 0.000 in all eight samples (the 5th percentile of electrons at 100 GeV is 14,167).

## 2. Reading, by the rule written in advance (registration section 5)

Under the registered rules the 3D route is **not available** for the development samples: the 3D estimator would need roughly 24,000 occupied voxels, more than any development electron at 100 GeV or below has and far more than the interacting protons have. It does **not** replace the 2D thresholds by a more permissive rule; it makes the eligibility problem worse (the 2D rule left 64 of 192 stage-1 tests testable; the 3D rule leaves none).

What this does not establish:
- That real showers lack finite-scale structure: it says that the estimator, with these controls and acceptance numbers, cannot be shown to work at these occupied-voxel counts.
- That the controls are the right ones. The result depends on the smooth baseline (a gamma-like depth profile with a sigma = 9 voxel lateral Gaussian core, which is itself strongly concentrated at the coarse scales, making the cascade's D0 - D2 smaller than the baseline's and the power zero there), on the equal-quanta hit model (a real shower has continuous energy deposits, so the number of "hits" and the number of occupied voxels are not the same thing as in the controls), and on the ordering rule (an interpretation added in 2D after the first run, awaiting the researcher). None of these was changed after seeing the numbers.
- The threshold is the median occupied voxels of the control image at the lowest usable hit count: using hit counts above 30,000 would raise it further, not lower it.

## 3. Consequences and options (the researcher decides; nothing is changed here)

1. The plan item "the 3D eligibility rule replaces the 336 / 572-cell thresholds" does not hold with the DEC-013 rules and these controls. The stage-1 to 3 tests stay limited to the 2D testable set (8 of 24 statistics, deposit only for matched pairs); detector-level survival of a finite-scale difference (P2) cannot be tested on the development data.
2. Options: (a) accept this and drop the claim that the fine-scale route is available below about 150 GeV, keeping P4 and P5 as the paper's anchors; (b) register a sensitivity of the controls (a less concentrated smooth baseline; the ordering rule restricted to windows without saturated scales) to learn how much of the negative result depends on the control design, reported beside this one and never as a replacement chosen after the fact; (c) look at higher-energy electrons (the occupied voxels grow roughly with energy: about 16,500 at 100 GeV, so a few hundred GeV would be needed), which fits an AMS-like spectrum up to the TeV but needs a production the researcher has not approved; (d) leave the finite-scale question to the readout-level statistics already tested and treat it as a secondary result.
3. For the amendment: P1 and P2 as drafted assume a 3D estimator that passes the controls; the draft should say so and name this result.
